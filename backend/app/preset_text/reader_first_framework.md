# Reader-First B2B Messaging Framework (DP)

DP does moments-based B2B marketing in Asia: standard playbooks, automated intent triggers and
modular content, instead of ad-hoc content. The messaging method borrows from Reader Response
Theory.

## Why "reader first"

1. **Meaning happens in the buyer's head, not in the copy.** A text has no fixed meaning until a
   reader brings their background, biases and situation to it. A CFO and a VP of Engineering
   reading the same landing page take away different things because they are under different
   pressures (cutting costs versus avoiding downtime).
2. **The implied reader is a buying persona in a specific moment.** Write for the exact mental
   state of a buyer at a moment (for example, a newly hired VP trying not to fail in their first
   90 days) and speak to their unspoken baggage, biases and scepticism.
3. **Gaps drive engagement.** Over-explaining features kills curiosity. State a sharp insight or
   problem and leave enough tension that the buyer connects it to their own company, and books a
   call to close the gap.

## The four lenses

### 1. The reader's horizon (horizon of expectations)
Every buyer arrives with baggage: past vendor burns, scepticism, industry assumptions.
- Define: what the buying committee already believes is true; which marketing clichés make them
  roll their eyes; how the first line acknowledges or disrupts those expectations.
- Sources: 2–3 star reviews on G2, Capterra and TrustRadius (what buyers hate about current
  solutions and which fluff they tune out); the top 3 competitors' homepages (write down phrases
  all three share, e.g. "streamline", "next-gen", "seamless").

### 2. The moment lens (situational context and reading posture)
How a buyer reads depends on when they see the message.
- Define: the trigger (just hired, failed an audit, Q4 budget freeze); the reading posture:
  skimming in panic (needs fast reassurance and checklists) or evaluating calmly (needs ROI maths
  and architecture proof).
- Sources: the "before" paragraph of client and competitor case studies (ignore the results;
  find the trigger event, e.g. "following an acquisition", "after failing a regional compliance
  audit"); Reddit and niche forums (r/sysadmin, r/CFO, r/salesoperations, regional forums).

### 3. The interpretive community (tribe language and career risk)
Buyers judge credibility in seconds by whether you speak their tribe's language.
- Define: 3–5 insider terms, metrics and workflows that prove we belong (e.g. SOC 2 Type II,
  churn rate, ERP sync; never "optimise operations"); the hidden career risk: what makes this
  role look bad in front of their boss.
- Rule: write like a peer sharing field notes, not a vendor pitching a brochure.
- Sources: 3–4 job descriptions for the target role. The responsibilities and requirements are
  usually written by the buyer's boss and state the KPIs they worry about.

### 4. The strategic gap (Iser's gaps and indeterminacy)
Great copy leaves an intentional space that pulls the reader forward.
- Reveal: the diagnostic insight or unexpected problem, stated clearly.
- Hold back: exactly how it applies to their setup, so booking a call is the natural way to
  close the loop.
- Rule: never answer every question in the ad or email; create enough tension to earn the next
  step.
- Source: the client's own deck.

## Buying stages

| Stage | The buyer's moment | Mindset and scepticism | What the copy must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status Quo Break | Something changes: an audit fails, a competitor moves, a new hire | "Is this bad enough to fix now, or can I ignore it?" | Validate the pain and create urgency. Use diagnostic hooks that show the hidden cost of doing nothing. |
| Solution Scramble | The buyer researches categories and builds a shortlist of 3–4 | "Every vendor sounds identical. Who won't waste my time?" | Differentiate through their baggage: call out where traditional tools fail and take a clear point of view. |
| Internal Sell | The champion pitches it to the CFO, IT and the rest of the committee | "What if I champion this and it fails? How do I justify the budget?" | Arm the champion: hard ROI maths, security specs and peer case studies instead of emotional hooks. |
| Final Hesitation | The contract is on the table, procurement reviews, cold feet | "What if implementation goes wrong? Can we switch smoothly?" | De-risk the decision: onboarding speed, migration support, low-friction guarantees. |

## Content kits

- **Pre-approved messaging framework** per client: core pains, compliance boundaries and brand
  voice agreed up front, so rapid-response posts and emails need minimal review.
- **Modular templates:** LinkedIn carousel, 3-email sequence, one-page executive briefing, short
  video script. Only the trigger hook and the proof change.
- **Always-on proof bank** per client: tagged ROI stats, customer quotes and security
  credentials, so the right proof can be pulled in minutes.
